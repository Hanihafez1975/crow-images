# Discussion: Lipid Profile Assessment

## 4.X Lipid Profile Assessment

The lipid profile findings of the present study provide compelling evidence for the deleterious effects of chronic deep-fried palm oil consumption on lipid homeostasis and underscore the therapeutic promise of *Alchemilla vulgaris*-mediated selenium nanoparticles (AV-SeNPs) as a novel anti-hypercholesterolemic intervention.

### 4.X.1 Deep-Fried Palm Oil–Induced Dyslipidemia

As illustrated in Figures (3), chronic exposure to deep-fried palm oil induced a pronounced dyslipidemic state in rats, characterized by marked elevations in serum total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), and low-density lipoprotein (LDL) levels (98.77%, 158.66%, and 98.34%, respectively), accompanied by a substantial reduction in high-density lipoprotein (HDL) levels (41.28%) relative to the normal control group (p < 0.05). These findings confirm the successful induction of hypercholesterolemia and profound disruption of lipid homeostasis following deep-fried palm oil consumption.

The dyslipidemic pattern observed in the DFP.OIL group is consistent with a growing body of recent evidence demonstrating that thermally oxidized and repeatedly heated palm oil profoundly disrupts lipid metabolism in experimental animals. Ogunlade et al. (2023) reported that deep-fried palm oil consumption induced significant dyslipidemia, oxidative stress, and hepatic damage in rats, with marked elevations in TC, TG, and LDL-C alongside reduced HDL-C levels (*Toxicology Reports*, 11:74–85; doi: 10.1016/j.toxrep.2023.07.001). Similarly, Afolabi et al. (2023) demonstrated that fried palm oil diets induced hypercholesterolemia and hepatic lipid accumulation in rats, attributing these effects to the generation of lipid peroxidation products and reactive oxygen species during the deep-frying process (*Food and Chemical Toxicology*, 177:113813; doi: 10.1016/j.fct.2023.113813). In a parallel investigation, Afolabi et al. (2024) confirmed that thermally oxidized palm oil diet-induced dyslipidemia was accompanied by perturbation of antioxidant defense systems and increased atherogenic indices in rats (*Food Science & Nutrition*, 12(9):6454–6466; doi: 10.1002/fsn3.4186). Furthermore, Ogunlade et al. (2024) extended these findings by demonstrating that thermally oxidized palm oil not only induced dyslipidemia but also caused significant hepatic injury and oxidative stress in rats (*Heliyon*, 10(8):e28413; doi: 10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e28413).

The mechanistic basis for deep-fried palm oil–induced dyslipidemia has been extensively reviewed by Leong et al. (2024), who provided a comprehensive narrative synthesis of evidence from both animal and human studies, concluding that repeated heating of palm oil generates toxic lipid oxidation products—including aldehydes, polar compounds, and polymeric triglycerides—that promote hepatic lipogenesis, impair cholesterol clearance via downregulation of LDL receptors, and exacerbate oxidative stress and vascular inflammation (*Frontiers in Nutrition*, 10:1313413; doi: 10.3389/fnut.2023.1313413). Jaarin et al. (2023) further demonstrated that repeatedly heated palm oil increased serum lipid peroxidation and impaired vascular reactivity in rats, reinforcing the cardiovascular risk associated with chronic consumption of thermally degraded cooking oils (*Lipids in Health and Disease*, 22(1):131; doi: 10.1186/s12944-023-01893-3). Additionally, Afolabi et al. (2024) showed that repeatedly heated palm oil consumption significantly increased the atherogenic index and caused hepatorenal damage in rats (*Scientific Reports*, 14(1):4713; doi: 10.1038/s41598-024-55413-3).

Collectively, these recent studies corroborate the present findings and reinforce the notion that deep-fried palm oil represents a significant dietary risk factor for the development of dyslipidemia, atherosclerosis, and associated cardiovascular complications. The magnitude of lipid perturbation observed in our DFP.OIL group—particularly the near-doubling of TC and LDL and the >150% increase in TG—is consistent with the severe dyslipidemic phenotype reported across these independent investigations.

### 4.X.2 Atorvastatin-Mediated Lipid Modulation

Administration of the standard hypolipidemic drug atorvastatin significantly attenuated deep-fried palm oil–induced dyslipidemia. Compared with the DFP.OIL group, atorvastatin treatment reduced serum TC, TG, and LDL levels by 44.34%, 44.32%, and 38.48%, respectively, while restoring HDL concentrations by 33.36%. Despite these improvements, lipid parameters in the DFP.OIL&ATOR group remained partially altered relative to the normal control group, indicating incomplete normalization of lipid metabolism.

The partial normalization observed with atorvastatin is a well-documented phenomenon in the literature. While statins are the gold standard for managing hypercholesterolemia through competitive inhibition of 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl coenzyme A (HMG-CoA) reductase, their efficacy in fully reversing diet-induced dyslipidemia—particularly in the context of thermally oxidized oil exposure—is often limited by the persistence of oxidative stress, hepatic inflammation, and residual lipid peroxidation products that are not directly addressed by the statin mechanism of action. This observation aligns with the concept of "residual cardiovascular risk" that persists despite optimal statin therapy, which has been attributed to elevated triglycerides, remnant lipoproteins, and ongoing inflammatory processes. The incomplete lipid normalization observed in the present study underscores the clinical need for complementary or alternative therapeutic strategies that can address multiple pathogenic pathways simultaneously—a rationale that supports the investigation of phytochemical-based and nanotechnological interventions.

### 4.X.3 Hypolipidemic Effects of *Alchemilla vulgaris* Extract

Oral administration of *Alchemilla vulgaris* (AV) extract exerted a notable hypolipidemic effect in hypercholesterolemic rats. Relative to the DFP.OIL group, AV treatment decreased TC, TG, and LDL levels by 34.52%, 46.30%, and 27.13%, respectively, while increasing HDL levels by 26.31%. Although these effects were substantial, lipid parameters remained modestly dysregulated compared with the control group.

The hypolipidemic activity of *A. vulgaris* observed in the present study is strongly supported by recent pharmacological investigations. Bouhrim et al. (2023) provided direct evidence that aqueous extracts of *A. vulgaris* (200 and 400 mg/kg) significantly reduced TC, TG, and LDL-C while increasing HDL-C in high-fat diet–induced hyperlipidemic rats, with concurrent hepatoprotective effects evidenced by reduced liver enzymes (AST and ALT) and improved hepatic antioxidant status (*Journal of Ethnopharmacology*, 303:115960; doi: 10.1016/j.jep.2022.115960). In a subsequent study, Bouhrim et al. (2023) further characterized the phytochemical composition of *A. vulgaris* extracts using HPLC-DAD analysis, identifying rich contents of flavonoids (particularly quercetin and kaempferol) and tannins (ellagitannins and gallotannins), and demonstrated significant anti-obesity and hypolipidemic properties in HFD-fed rats, with the aqueous extract at 400 mg/kg showing the most pronounced lipid-lowering effects (*Pharmaceuticals*, 16(9):1283; doi: 10.3390/ph16091283).

More recently, Bouhrim et al. (2024) investigated *A. vulgaris* extract as a potential therapeutic agent for metabolic syndrome management in HFD-fed rats, demonstrating that the extract significantly reduced body weight gain, visceral fat accumulation, and improved lipid profiles by decreasing TC, TG, and LDL-C while increasing HDL-C. The extract also improved glucose tolerance, reduced fasting blood glucose, enhanced insulin sensitivity, and exhibited significant antioxidant activity (*Pharmaceuticals*, 17(10):1371; doi: 10.3390/ph17101371). A comprehensive review by Bouhrim et al. (2024) consolidated the existing evidence on *A. vulgaris*, highlighting its rich bioactive compound profile—including flavonoids, tannins, phenolic acids, and triterpenoids—and its demonstrated efficacy in managing diabetes, obesity, gastrointestinal disorders, and lipid metabolism regulation (*Plants*, 13(23):3276; doi: 10.3390/plants13233276).

The lipid-lowering mechanisms of *A. vulgaris* are likely multifactorial and may include: (i) inhibition of pancreatic lipase activity, thereby reducing intestinal lipid absorption; (ii) modulation of hepatic lipid metabolism through regulation of lipogenic and lipolytic gene expression (e.g., SREBP-1c, FAS, PPARα); (iii) enhancement of fecal cholesterol and bile acid excretion; and (iv) potent antioxidant activity that mitigates lipid peroxidation and preserves hepatocyte integrity. The flavonoid-rich composition of *A. vulgaris*—particularly quercetin and kaempferol—has been independently shown to inhibit HMG-CoA reductase activity and upregulate hepatic LDL receptor expression, providing a mechanistic basis for the observed reductions in TC and LDL-C.

### 4.X.4 Superior Lipid-Modulating Efficacy of AV-Mediated Selenium Nanoparticles

Remarkably, selenium nanoparticles biosynthesized using *Alchemilla vulgaris* (AV-SeNPs) demonstrated the most potent lipid-modulating efficacy among all treatment groups. Compared with the DFP.OIL group, AV-SeNPs administration reduced serum TC, TG, and LDL levels by 50.16%, 56.10%, and 52.96%, respectively, while restoring HDL concentrations by 39.68%. Furthermore, lipid profile parameters in the DFP.OIL&AV-SeNPs group were significantly improved compared with the atorvastatin-treated group and closely approximated those of the normal control group, highlighting the superior anti-hypercholesterolemic and hepatoprotective potential of AV-mediated selenium nanoparticles.

The superior efficacy of AV-SeNPs over both the crude *A. vulgaris* extract and atorvastatin represents a particularly noteworthy finding. This enhanced activity can be attributed to the synergistic interplay between the intrinsic biological properties of selenium at the nanoscale and the bioactive phytoconstituents of *A. vulgaris* that serve as both reducing and capping agents during the green synthesis process.

Recent literature strongly supports the hypolipidemic potential of biogenic selenium nanoparticles. Abdulmalek and Balbaa (2024) demonstrated that SeNPs significantly reduced serum TC, TG, and LDL-C while increasing HDL-C in streptozotocin-induced diabetic rats, with concurrent cardioprotective effects including reduced oxidative stress markers and decreased inflammatory cytokines (*Heliyon*, 10(2):e24267; doi: 10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e24267). Hashem et al. (2025) reported that SeNPs synthesized using *Lactobacillus acidophilus*-derived exopolysaccharides exhibited significant anti-hyperlipidemic activity in hyperlipidemic rats, with marked reductions in TC, TG, and LDL-C and increases in HDL-C (*BMC Microbiology*, 25(1):148; doi: 10.1186/s12866-025-03838-z). In a series of comprehensive studies, Ren et al. (2023–2025) demonstrated that selenium nanoparticles-enriched *Lactobacillus casei* ATCC 393 ameliorated high-fat diet–induced hepatic steatosis, obesity, and metabolic dysfunction in mice by modulating lipid metabolism-related gene expression (SREBP-1c, FAS, PPARα, CPT-1) and improving gut microbiota composition (*Journal of the Science of Food and Agriculture*, 2025, doi: 10.1002/jsfa.14268; *Journal of Dairy Science*, 2024, 107(9):6405–6420, doi: 10.3168/jds.2024-24579; *Food Function*, 2024, 15(12):6594–6607).

Particularly relevant to the present study, Alharbi and Alsubhi (2025) demonstrated that biogenic SeNPs synthesized using *Moringa oleifera* leaf extract—a green synthesis approach analogous to our AV-mediated biosynthesis—exhibited potent protective effects against high-fat diet–induced hyperlipidemia in rats, with significant improvements in lipid profiles, antioxidant status, and hepatorenal function (*Scientific Reports*, 15(1):4571; doi: 10.1038/s41598-025-88893-w; *Frontiers in Pharmacology*, 15:1504879; doi: 10.3389/fphar.2024.1504879). Similarly, Amin et al. (2024) reported that biogenic SeNPs from *Pleurotus ostreatus* demonstrated significant hypolipidemic, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory activities in hyperlipidemic rats (*Scientific Reports*, 14(1):17079; doi: 10.1038/s41598-024-67893-0). Notably, Amin et al. (2024) also demonstrated that biogenic SeNPs enhanced the hypolipidemic effect of lovastatin, suggesting a synergistic potential between SeNPs and conventional lipid-lowering drugs (*Scientific Reports*, 14(1):18893; doi: 10.1038/s41598-024-69780-0). Furthermore, Abdel-Hady et al. (2024) confirmed the anti-hyperlipidemic activities of biogenic SeNPs synthesized by *Lactobacillus paracasei* (*Biological Trace Element Research*, 202(12):5637–5651; doi: 10.1007/s12011-024-04413-z), while Hashem et al. (2024) and Elbagory et al. (2024) reported similar anti-hyperlipidemic effects for SeNPs biosynthesized from *Penicillium chrysogenum* and *Aspergillus terreus*, respectively (*Scientific Reports*, 14(1):11133, doi: 10.1038/s41598-024-61970-6; 14(1):22780, doi: 10.1038/s41598-024-73879-5).

### 4.X.5 Mechanistic Considerations for the Superior Efficacy of AV-SeNPs

The superior lipid-modulating efficacy of AV-SeNPs compared with both the crude AV extract and atorvastatin can be rationalized through several complementary mechanisms:

**First**, selenium nanoparticles possess inherent antioxidant properties that are substantially enhanced at the nanoscale due to their high surface area-to-volume ratio, which facilitates greater bioavailability and cellular uptake compared with bulk selenium or selenite salts. Selenium is an essential component of glutathione peroxidase (GPx) and other selenoproteins that play critical roles in mitigating oxidative stress—a key driver of lipid peroxidation and dyslipidemia. By restoring the antioxidant defense system, SeNPs can interrupt the vicious cycle of oxidative stress–mediated hepatic lipid accumulation and cholesterol dysregulation (Yadav et al., 2024; *Heliyon*, 10(23):e40218; doi: 10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e40218; Alipour et al., 2023; *Alexandria Engineering Journal*, doi: 10.1016/j.aej.2023.09.046).

**Second**, the green synthesis of SeNPs using *A. vulgaris* extract ensures that the nanoparticles are capped and stabilized by the plant's bioactive phytoconstituents—including quercetin, kaempferol, ellagitannins, and phenolic acids—which themselves possess independent hypolipidemic, anti-inflammatory, and hepatoprotective activities. This biogenic capping creates a multifunctional nanocomposite that delivers both selenium-mediated and phytochemical-mediated therapeutic effects simultaneously, resulting in a synergistic enhancement of lipid-lowering activity that exceeds the sum of individual contributions.

**Third**, selenium nanoparticles have been shown to modulate key lipid metabolism pathways, including downregulation of sterol regulatory element-binding protein-1c (SREBP-1c) and fatty acid synthase (FAS)—master regulators of hepatic de novo lipogenesis—and upregulation of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha (PPARα) and carnitine palmitoyltransferase-1 (CPT-1), which promote fatty acid β-oxidation (Ren et al., 2025; *J Sci Food Agric*, doi: 10.1002/jsfa.14268). This dual modulation of lipogenic and lipolytic pathways provides a mechanistic explanation for the pronounced reductions in TC and TG observed in the AV-SeNPs group.

**Fourth**, emerging evidence suggests that selenium nanoparticles can favorably modulate gut microbiota composition, increasing beneficial bacteria (e.g., *Lactobacillus*, *Bifidobacterium*) while decreasing harmful bacteria, thereby influencing bile acid metabolism and cholesterol homeostasis through the gut–liver axis (Ren et al., 2025; *Food Function*, 16(4):1592–1607; doi: 10.1039/d4fo05024a).

**Fifth**, the near-complete normalization of HDL-C levels in the AV-SeNPs group (39.68% increase relative to DFP.OIL) suggests that AV-SeNPs may enhance reverse cholesterol transport—the process by which excess cholesterol is transported from peripheral tissues back to the liver for excretion—potentially through upregulation of apolipoprotein A-I (ApoA-I) and ATP-binding cassette transporter A1 (ABCA1) expression.

### 4.X.6 Comparative Analysis and Clinical Implications

The finding that AV-SeNPs outperformed atorvastatin—the clinical gold standard for hypercholesterolemia management—in normalizing lipid parameters is of considerable translational significance. While atorvastatin primarily targets HMG-CoA reductase to reduce hepatic cholesterol synthesis, AV-SeNPs appear to modulate multiple pathogenic pathways simultaneously, including oxidative stress, hepatic lipogenesis, fatty acid oxidation, inflammation, and potentially gut microbiota composition. This multi-target mechanism of action may explain the superior efficacy observed and positions AV-SeNPs as a promising candidate for the development of novel, plant-based nanotherapeutics for the management of dyslipidemia and associated metabolic disorders.

Moreover, the close approximation of lipid parameters in the AV-SeNPs group to those of the normal control group suggests that AV-SeNPs may achieve near-complete reversal of deep-fried palm oil–induced dyslipidemia—a therapeutic outcome that was not attained by either atorvastatin or the crude AV extract alone. This observation is consistent with the emerging paradigm in nanomedicine that biogenic nanoparticles synthesized using medicinal plant extracts can serve as superior therapeutic agents compared with both conventional drugs and crude plant extracts, owing to their enhanced bioavailability, targeted delivery, and synergistic bioactivity.
