## Supplementary Methods

### S1. Nanoparticle Characterization

Chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole nanoparticles were characterized for:
- **Particle size and polydispersity index (PDI):** Dynamic light scattering (DLS) using Malvern Zetasizer Nano ZS
- **Zeta potential:** Electrophoretic light scattering using Malvern Zetasizer Nano ZS
- **Morphology:** Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) using JEOL JEM-2100
- **Encapsulation efficiency:** UV-visible spectrophotometry at 280 nm
- **Drug loading capacity:** Calculated as (mass of drug in nanoparticles / mass of nanoparticles) × 100%

## Supplementary Discussion

### S1. Mechanisms of pH-Responsive Drug Release

The pH-responsive behavior of chitosan nanoparticles is governed by the pKa of chitosan's amino groups (~6.5). At acidic pH below the pKa, amino groups become protonated (NH3+), leading to:

1. **Increased electrostatic repulsion** between positively charged chitosan chains
2. **Nanoparticle swelling** due to osmotic pressure from counterions
3. **Enhanced drug diffusion** through the expanded polymer network
4. **Potential nanoparticle dissolution** at very low pH

At neutral to alkaline pH above the pKa, amino groups are largely deprotonated (NH2), resulting in:

1. **Reduced electrostatic repulsion** and compact nanoparticle structure
2. **Stronger hydrogen bonding** between chitosan chains
3. **Reduced drug diffusion** through the dense polymer network
4. **Sustained drug retention** within nanoparticles

### S2. Temporal Dynamics of Molecular Responses

The time-dependent changes in molecular markers reveal distinct temporal phases:

**Early Phase (12h):**
- Maximum drug release at acidic pH
- Peak caspase expression indicating acute apoptotic response
- Relatively suppressed PI3K/AKT signaling
- Moderate P53 activation

**Intermediate Phase (24h):**
- Sustained drug release
- Peak P53 expression coordinating stress response
- Emerging PI3K/AKT activation as compensatory mechanism
- Declining caspase expression possibly due to cell death

**Late Phase (36h):**
- Reduced drug release at acidic pH (possible degradation)
- Maximum PI3K/AKT activation across all pH conditions
- Further decline in caspase expression
- Reduced P53 expression

### S3. Implications for Combination Therapy

The time-dependent upregulation of PI3K/AKT survival signaling suggests potential therapeutic resistance mechanisms. Combination strategies could include:

1. **PI3K/AKT inhibitors** (e.g., alpelisib, capivasertib) to block compensatory survival signaling
2. **Bcl-2 inhibitors** (e.g., venetoclax) to enhance apoptotic sensitivity
3. **P53 activators** (e.g., APR-246) to amplify tumor suppressor function
4. **Antioxidants** to modulate selenium-induced oxidative stress
5. **Autophagy modulators** to prevent autophagic survival responses

### S4. Clinical Translation Considerations

Key considerations for clinical translation include:

1. **Formulation optimization:** Scale-up manufacturing, stability testing, quality control
2. **Pharmacokinetics:** Absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion (ADME) studies
3. **Biodistribution:** Tumor accumulation, normal tissue distribution, clearance pathways
4. **Toxicology:** Acute and chronic toxicity, maximum tolerated dose, dose-limiting toxicities
5. **Efficacy:** In vivo tumor growth inhibition, survival benefit, metastasis prevention
6. **Biomarkers:** Identification of predictive and pharmacodynamic biomarkers
7. **Patient selection:** Tumor pH profiling, molecular subtyping, personalized medicine approaches
