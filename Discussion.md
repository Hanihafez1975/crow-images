# Discussion

The present study provides compelling evidence that green-synthesized selenium nanoparticles using *Alchemilla vulgaris* extract (AV-SeNPs) confer superior multi-targeted protection against deep-fried palm oil–induced hypercholesterolemia and hepatic injury in rats, outperforming the individual components (*A. vulgaris* extract and sodium selenite) as well as the conventional lipid-lowering drug atorvastatin. These findings are discussed in the context of current literature and mechanistic understanding below.

---

## 1. Deep-Fried Palm Oil as a Model of Diet-Induced Metabolic Injury

Repeated thermal processing of palm oil generates a complex mixture of toxic lipid oxidation products, including aldehydes, peroxides, polar compounds, and polymeric triglycerides, which are well-documented inducers of dyslipidemia, oxidative stress, and organ damage (Jaarin & Kamisah, 2012; Falade et al., 2017). In the current study, rats fed deep-fried palm oil developed marked dyslipidemia characterized by elevated total cholesterol, triglycerides, and LDL-cholesterol, alongside reduced HDL-cholesterol. These observations are consistent with previous reports demonstrating that thermally oxidized oils disrupt hepatic cholesterol homeostasis by upregulating HMG-CoA reductase activity and impairing LDL receptor–mediated clearance (Soriguer et al., 2003; Leong et al., 2015). Furthermore, the significant elevation of serum hepatic enzymes (ALT, AST, and ALP) confirmed hepatocellular injury, corroborating earlier findings that oxidized dietary lipids compromise hepatocyte membrane integrity and promote necroinflammatory changes (Ani et al., 2015; Owu et al., 2018).

## 2. Characterization and Rationale for AV-SeNPs

The green synthesis of selenium nanoparticles using *A. vulgaris* ethanolic extract represents a biologically and environmentally favorable approach to nanomaterial fabrication. The characterization data obtained by DLS, zeta potential analysis, TEM, and FTIR confirmed the successful formation of stable, well-dispersed nanoparticles with surface functionalization by phytochemical moieties from *A. vulgaris*. This is in agreement with the growing body of literature supporting the use of plant-derived biomolecules as both reducing and capping agents in nanoparticle biosynthesis, which enhances biocompatibility, stability, and biological activity (Menon et al., 2019; Khurana et al., 2019). The phytochemical richness of *A. vulgaris*—including flavonoids, tannins, phenolic acids, and triterpenoids—provides an ideal matrix for nanoparticle functionalization, potentially synergizing with the intrinsic bioactivities of selenium (Boroja et al., 2018; Takó et al., 2020).

## 3. Hypolipidemic Efficacy of AV-SeNPs

A central finding of this study is the superior hypolipidemic efficacy of AV-SeNPs compared with AV, Na₂SeO₃, and atorvastatin. AV-SeNPs normalized serum lipid parameters more effectively than all comparators, suggesting a synergistic interaction between the selenium core and the phytochemical corona. Selenium has been shown to modulate lipid metabolism through the activation of selenoproteins, particularly glutathione peroxidase (GPx), which mitigates lipid peroxidation and preserves lipoprotein structural integrity (Rayman, 2012; Steinbrenner et al., 2016). Meanwhile, the polyphenolic constituents of *A. vulgaris* are known to inhibit intestinal cholesterol absorption, enhance fecal bile acid excretion, and suppress hepatic lipogenesis via AMPK/SREBP-1c signaling (Choi et al., 2017; Boroja et al., 2018). The nanoparticulate delivery system likely enhanced the bioavailability and cellular uptake of both selenium and phytochemicals, as has been reported for other biogenic selenium nanoparticles (Hosnedlova et al., 2018; Ferro et al., 2021). Notably, the superior performance of AV-SeNPs over atorvastatin—a potent HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor—underscores the multi-mechanistic nature of the nanoformulation, which likely targets multiple nodes in lipid metabolic pathways simultaneously.

## 4. Hepatoprotective Effects and Restoration of Liver Enzyme Activities

The restoration of serum ALT, AST, and ALP activities by AV-SeNPs to near-normal levels indicates robust hepatoprotection. This finding aligns with previous studies demonstrating the hepatoprotective potential of both selenium supplementation and *A. vulgaris* extract in various models of liver injury (Bera et al., 2013; Shaker et al., 2019). The hepatoprotective mechanism of AV-SeNPs likely involves the stabilization of hepatocyte membranes, suppression of lipid peroxidation-mediated membrane damage, and preservation of mitochondrial function. Selenium nanoparticles have been shown to accumulate preferentially in the liver, where they exert direct cytoprotective effects through selenoprotein-dependent and -independent mechanisms (Zhang et al., 2020; Varlamova et al., 2021). The histopathological findings further corroborated the biochemical data, with AV-SeNPs preserving hepatic architecture and preventing the fatty degeneration, inflammatory infiltration, and necrotic changes observed in the deep-fried palm oil–fed group.

## 5. Modulation of Oxidative and Nitrosative Stress

Oxidative stress is a pivotal mechanism linking dietary oxidized lipid consumption to hepatic injury (Ito et al., 2019). In the present study, deep-fried palm oil induced significant elevations in hepatic MDA (a marker of lipid peroxidation), nitric oxide (NO), and inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS), reflecting both oxidative and nitrosative stress. AV-SeNPs demonstrated the most potent suppression of these markers among all treatment groups. The antioxidant efficacy of AV-SeNPs can be attributed to the robust activation of the Nrf2 (nuclear factor erythroid 2–related factor 2) signaling pathway, which was significantly upregulated in the AV-SeNPs–treated group. Nrf2 is a master transcriptional regulator of antioxidant and cytoprotective genes, including GPx, heme oxygenase-1 (HMOX-1), and other phase II detoxification enzymes (Ma, 2013; Bellezza et al., 2018). The activation of Nrf2 by AV-SeNPs is consistent with reports that both selenium species and polyphenolic compounds can independently activate Nrf2 through Keap1 modification and ARE-mediated transcription (Hybertson et al., 2011; Wang et al., 2019). The synergistic activation observed with AV-SeNPs suggests that the nanoformulation amplifies Nrf2 signaling beyond what either component achieves alone, providing a more comprehensive antioxidant defense.

The upregulation of GPx activity by AV-SeNPs is particularly noteworthy, as GPx is a selenium-dependent enzyme that catalyzes the reduction of hydrogen peroxide and lipid hydroperoxides, thereby preventing oxidative chain reactions and ferroptotic cell death (Brigelius-Flohé & Maiorino, 2013). Similarly, the induction of HMOX-1—a stress-responsive enzyme that degrades heme into biliverdin, carbon monoxide, and free iron—confers additional cytoprotection through anti-inflammatory, anti-apoptotic, and antioxidant mechanisms (Ryter, 2021).

## 6. Anti-Inflammatory Effects and NF-κB Suppression

Chronic low-grade inflammation is a hallmark of diet-induced metabolic disorders and plays a central role in the progression of hepatic steatosis to steatohepatitis and fibrosis (Tilg & Moschen, 2010; Kazankov et al., 2019). The present study demonstrated that deep-fried palm oil consumption significantly elevated hepatic levels of pro-inflammatory mediators, including tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB). AV-SeNPs exhibited the most pronounced anti-inflammatory effects, suppressing all three mediators more effectively than AV, Na₂SeO₃, and atorvastatin.

The anti-inflammatory mechanism of AV-SeNPs likely involves the dual suppression of NF-κB activation and the upstream oxidative stress signals that drive inflammatory gene transcription. NF-κB is a redox-sensitive transcription factor that, upon activation by reactive oxygen species (ROS), translocates to the nucleus and induces the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and adhesion molecules (Liu et al., 2017). By simultaneously quenching ROS through Nrf2/GPx activation and directly inhibiting NF-κB nuclear translocation, AV-SeNPs interrupt the vicious cycle of oxidative stress and inflammation that perpetuates hepatic injury. This dual mechanism has been described for selenium nanoparticles in other disease models (Khurana et al., 2019; Maiyo & Singh, 2020) and is further enhanced by the well-documented anti-inflammatory properties of *A. vulgaris* polyphenols, which inhibit cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), lipoxygenase, and MAPK signaling cascades (Boroja et al., 2018; Takó et al., 2020).

## 7. Anti-Apoptotic Effects and Bax/Bcl-2 Balance

Apoptosis of hepatocytes is a critical event in the pathogenesis of diet-induced liver injury, driven by oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and inflammatory signaling (Guicciardi & Gores, 2005; Luedde et al., 2014). The present study revealed that deep-fried palm oil consumption disrupted the Bax/Bcl-2 balance, favoring pro-apoptotic signaling. AV-SeNPs effectively restored this balance by downregulating the pro-apoptotic protein Bax and upregulating the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2, indicating potent anti-apoptotic activity.

The anti-apoptotic mechanism of AV-SeNPs is likely multifactorial. First, the suppression of oxidative stress by AV-SeNPs prevents mitochondrial membrane depolarization and cytochrome c release, thereby inhibiting the intrinsic apoptotic pathway (Orrenius et al., 2007). Second, the inhibition of NF-κB and TNF-α signaling by AV-SeNPs attenuates the extrinsic apoptotic pathway mediated by death receptors (Wajant et al., 2003). Third, the activation of Nrf2 by AV-SeNPs has been shown to directly upregulate Bcl-2 expression and suppress Bax, providing transcriptional-level anti-apoptotic protection (Niture & Bhargavan, 2012). These findings are consistent with previous reports demonstrating the anti-apoptotic effects of selenium nanoparticles in hepatotoxicity models (Varlamova et al., 2021; Pi et al., 2020).

## 8. Sirt-1 Expression and Metabolic Regulation

An important and novel finding of this study is the normalization of Sirt-1 (sirtuin 1) expression by AV-SeNPs. Sirt-1 is an NAD⁺-dependent deacetylase that plays a central role in metabolic regulation, including lipid metabolism, glucose homeostasis, inflammation, and cellular stress responses (Houtkooper et al., 2012; Ding et al., 2017). Sirt-1 deacetylates and activates key transcription factors such as PGC-1α, FOXO, and LXR, promoting fatty acid oxidation, gluconeogenesis, and cholesterol efflux (Li, 2013). The downregulation of Sirt-1 by deep-fried palm oil consumption observed in this study is consistent with reports that oxidative stress and high-fat diets suppress Sirt-1 expression, contributing to metabolic dysregulation and hepatic steatosis (Colak et al., 2014; Nassir & Bhargava, 2018).

The restoration of Sirt-1 expression by AV-SeNPs suggests that the nanoformulation activates a metabolic reprogramming cascade that enhances lipid catabolism and suppresses lipogenesis. Furthermore, Sirt-1 has been shown to deacetylate and activate Nrf2, creating a positive feedback loop that amplifies antioxidant defenses (Huang et al., 2017). Sirt-1 also deacetylates the p65 subunit of NF-κB, suppressing its transcriptional activity and thereby reducing inflammation (Yeung et al., 2004). Thus, the normalization of Sirt-1 by AV-SeNPs may represent a central upstream mechanism through which the nanoformulation exerts its pleiotropic protective effects.

## 9. Superiority of AV-SeNPs Over Individual Components and Atorvastatin

A critical observation across all parameters examined is the consistent superiority of AV-SeNPs over AV extract alone, Na₂SeO₃ alone, and atorvastatin. This superiority can be attributed to several factors:

**First**, the nanoparticulate form of selenium offers enhanced bioavailability, lower toxicity, and improved cellular uptake compared with inorganic selenium salts such as Na₂SeO₃ (Hosnedlova et al., 2018; Ferro et al., 2021). Selenium nanoparticles exhibit a wider therapeutic window, as they maintain biological activity at doses where inorganic selenium would be toxic (Zhang et al., 2020).

**Second**, the biogenic synthesis using *A. vulgaris* extract results in surface functionalization of the nanoparticles with bioactive phytochemicals, creating a hybrid nanomaterial that combines the selenoprotein-inducing capacity of selenium with the polyphenol-mediated antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and metabolic regulatory activities of *A. vulgaris* (Menon et al., 2019; Khurana et al., 2019).

**Third**, the nanoparticulate delivery system may facilitate targeted hepatic accumulation due to the liver's role as the primary organ for nanoparticle clearance via the reticuloendothelial system, thereby concentrating the therapeutic payload at the site of injury (Tsoi et al., 2016).

**Fourth**, unlike atorvastatin, which primarily targets HMG-CoA reductase in a single-mechanism approach, AV-SeNPs simultaneously modulate multiple pathological pathways—including lipid metabolism, oxidative stress (Nrf2/GPx/HMOX-1), inflammation (NF-κB/TNF-α/IL-6), apoptosis (Bax/Bcl-2), and metabolic regulation (Sirt-1)—providing comprehensive, multi-targeted protection (Steinbrenner et al., 2016; Bellezza et al., 2018).

## 10. Clinical and Translational Implications

The findings of this study carry significant translational implications. The global prevalence of dyslipidemia and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) continues to rise, driven in part by the widespread consumption of repeatedly heated cooking oils in both domestic and commercial food preparation (Ng et al., 2014; Younossi et al., 2019). Current pharmacological interventions, particularly statins, while effective in lowering LDL-cholesterol, are associated with adverse effects including myopathy, hepatotoxicity, and new-onset diabetes, limiting their long-term use in certain populations (Ward et al., 2019; Sirtori, 2014). The development of safe, nature-based alternatives or adjuncts to conventional therapy is therefore of considerable clinical interest.

AV-SeNPs, as demonstrated in this study, offer a promising candidate for such an approach. Their multi-targeted mechanism of action, favorable safety profile inherent to biogenic nanoparticles, and the established traditional use of *A. vulgaris* in folk medicine provide a strong foundation for further preclinical and clinical development (Boroja et al., 2018; Hosnedlova et al., 2018). However, several aspects warrant further investigation, including dose-response optimization, long-term safety assessment, pharmacokinetic profiling, and evaluation in other models of metabolic syndrome and liver disease.

## 11. Limitations and Future Directions

While the present study provides robust evidence for the protective efficacy of AV-SeNPs, certain limitations should be acknowledged. The study was conducted in a rodent model, and species-specific differences in lipid metabolism and drug disposition may limit direct extrapolation to humans. Additionally, the precise molecular interactions between the selenium core and the phytochemical corona of AV-SeNPs remain to be fully elucidated using advanced proteomic and metabolomic approaches. Future studies should also explore the gut microbiome–mediated effects of AV-SeNPs, as both selenium and polyphenols are known to modulate gut microbial composition and function, which in turn influences systemic lipid metabolism and inflammation (Kasaikina et al., 2011; Cardona et al., 2013). Furthermore, clinical trials in human subjects with dyslipidemia and NAFLD are warranted to validate the translational potential of this novel nanoformulation.

---

## Conclusion

In conclusion, the present study demonstrates that green-synthesized AV-SeNPs provide potent, multi-targeted protection against deep-fried palm oil–induced hypercholesterolemia and hepatic injury through the synergistic modulation of lipid metabolism, oxidative stress (Nrf2/GPx/HMOX-1 axis), inflammation (NF-κB/TNF-α/IL-6 pathway), apoptosis (Bax/Bcl-2 balance), and metabolic regulation (Sirt-1). The consistent superiority of AV-SeNPs over *A. vulgaris* extract, sodium selenite, and atorvastatin highlights the therapeutic advantage of the biogenic nanoformulation and underscores its promise as a safe, effective, nature-based alternative or adjunct to conventional lipid-lowering therapy.
