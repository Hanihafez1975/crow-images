# Professional Scientific Abstract

**Title:** Selenium nanoparticles, enhanced by kaempferol, mitigate acute kidney injury caused by rhabdomyolysis through antioxidant and anti-inflammatory actions depending on the NRF2/NF-κB pathway

---

## Abstract

**Background:** Acute kidney injury (AKI) remains a critical clinical condition associated with significant morbidity and mortality, for which effective therapeutic strategies are urgently needed. Rhabdomyolysis-induced AKI (RIAKI) involves myoglobin-mediated tubular obstruction, oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis, representing a well-established experimental model for evaluating renoprotective interventions.

**Objective:** This study aimed to investigate and compare the renoprotective efficacy of kaempferol (KMP), sodium selenite (Na₂SeO₃), and kaempferol-mediated selenium nanoparticles (KMP-SeNPs) against glycerol-induced RIAKI in rats, with emphasis on the NRF2/NF-κB signaling axis.

**Methods:** KMP-SeNPs were synthesized via a green reduction approach using kaempferol as both bio-reductant and stabilizing agent, yielding spherical nanoparticles (Z-average: 94.8 nm; zeta potential: −67.5 mV) confirmed by DLS, TEM, FTIR, and UV-Vis spectroscopy. Thirty-five male Wistar rats were randomly allocated into five groups (n = 7): control, AKI, AKI+KMP (50 mg/kg/day), AKI+Na₂SeO₃ (0.5 mg/kg/day), and AKI+KMP-SeNPs (0.5 mg/kg/day). Treatments were administered orally for 21 days, followed by intramuscular glycerol injection (50%, 10 mg/kg) on day 20 to induce RIAKI. Renal function biomarkers (creatinine, BUN, cystatin-C, KIM-1), oxidative stress indices (NGAL, 8-OHdG, MDA, SOD, CAT, GSH, GPx-4), inflammatory mediators (TNF-α, IL-6, NF-κB, IL-1β), and apoptotic markers (Bax, Bcl-2, caspase-3) were assessed. Histopathological examination and immunohistochemistry for Nrf2 and FOXP3 were performed.

**Results:** Glycerol administration induced severe AKI, evidenced by significant elevations in renal function markers, oxidative stress indices, pro-inflammatory cytokines, and pro-apoptotic proteins, with concurrent suppression of antioxidant enzymes and anti-apoptotic Bcl-2. While KMP and Na₂SeO₃ pretreatment conferred partial renoprotection, KMP-SeNPs demonstrated markedly superior efficacy, nearly normalizing renal function biomarkers, restoring redox homeostasis, suppressing NF-κB-mediated inflammatory signaling (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β reductions of 57.91%, 60.44%, and 65.38%, respectively), and attenuating mitochondrial-dependent apoptosis (Bax and caspase-3 reductions of 59.71% and 63.84%; Bcl-2 restoration of 94.36%). Histopathological evaluation confirmed preservation of renal and skeletal muscle architecture in the KMP-SeNPs group. Immunohistochemical analysis revealed restored Nrf2 and FOXP3 expression, indicating enhanced antioxidant defense and immunoregulatory capacity. Principal component analysis corroborated that KMP-SeNPs achieved the greatest separation from the injury phenotype toward a protective molecular signature.

**Conclusion:** KMP-SeNPs exert superior renoprotective effects compared with KMP or Na₂SeO₃ alone against RIAKI, mediated through synergistic modulation of the NRF2/NF-κB pathway, attenuation of oxidative stress-driven inflammation, and inhibition of apoptosis. These findings position KMP-SeNPs as a promising nanotherapeutic candidate for the management of rhabdomyolysis-induced AKI.

**Keywords:** Acute kidney injury; Kaempferol; Selenium nanoparticles; Oxidative stress; Inflammation; Apoptosis; Nrf2; NF-κB; FOXP3
