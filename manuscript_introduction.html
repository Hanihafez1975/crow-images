Introduction

Lung Adenocarcinoma: A Global Health Challenge

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with lung adenocarcinoma representing the most prevalent histological subtype of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Despite significant advances in targeted therapies and immunotherapy, the five-year survival rate for advanced-stage lung adenocarcinoma remains disappointingly low, primarily due to intrinsic and acquired therapeutic resistance mechanisms. The complex molecular landscape of lung adenocarcinoma, characterized by aberrant activation of multiple oncogenic signaling pathways and dysregulation of cellular stress response systems, necessitates the development of novel multi-targeted therapeutic strategies.

Selenium-Containing Compounds: Emerging Anticancer Agents

Selenium, an essential trace element, has garnered considerable attention in cancer research due to its unique biochemical properties and potential anticancer activities. Epidemiological studies have demonstrated an inverse correlation between selenium levels and cancer incidence, with lung cancer patients exhibiting significantly lower serum selenium concentrations compared to healthy individuals. This observation has catalyzed extensive research into selenium-based compounds as potential chemotherapeutic and chemopreventive agents.

Among selenium-containing heterocycles, selenadiazole derivatives have emerged as particularly promising candidates for anticancer drug development. The benzo[c][1,2,5]selenadiazole scaffold has demonstrated potent anti-proliferative activity against various cancer cell lines, including lung cancer (HTB-54), with certain derivatives exhibiting GI₅₀ values as low as 3.7 μM and selectivity indices exceeding 27.1 (Plano et al., 2018). Notably, these compounds induce cell death through mechanisms independent of classical apoptotic pathways, suggesting novel therapeutic potential for overcoming apoptosis-resistant cancer phenotypes.

Functional Group Modifications: Imino and Sulphone Derivatives

The incorporation of imino and sulphone functional groups into selenadiazole frameworks represents a rational drug design strategy aimed at enhancing anticancer selectivity and potency. Recent investigations have demonstrated that 2-alkylthio-4-chloro-N-[imino(heteroaryl)methyl]benzenesulfonamide derivatives exhibit remarkable selectivity for cancer cells, with IC₅₀ values of 6-7 μM in HeLa cells while maintaining approximately three-fold lower toxicity toward normal HaCaT cells (IC₅₀: 18-20 μM) (Kovalenko et al., 2023). This selective cytotoxicity profile is particularly advantageous for minimizing treatment-related adverse effects.

Furthermore, imidoselenocarbamate derivatives have been shown to release methylselenol under hypoxic tumor microenvironments, a highly reactive selenium species responsible for pro-apoptotic and antiproliferative activities in H157 lung cancer cells (Sanmartín et al., 2012). The controlled delivery of methylselenol represents a sophisticated approach to exploiting the unique metabolic characteristics of solid tumors, including lung adenocarcinoma.

The integration of selenium with sulfonamide scaffolds leverages the well-established pharmacological properties of sulfonamide-containing drugs while introducing the redox-modulating capabilities of selenium. This hybrid molecular architecture facilitates multiple mechanisms of action, including reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation, thiol/disulfide balance disruption, and modulation of cancer-related transcription factors such as NF-κB, p53, and AP-1 (Fernandes and Gandin, 2015).

Heat Shock Proteins: Guardians of Cancer Cell Survival

Heat shock proteins (HSPs) constitute a family of highly conserved molecular chaperones that play pivotal roles in protein homeostasis, cellular stress responses, and cancer biology. In lung adenocarcinoma, HSP60, HSP70, and HSP90 are frequently overexpressed and contribute to tumor initiation, progression, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance.

HSP90: Master Regulator of Oncogenic Signaling

HSP90 represents one of the most extensively studied molecular chaperones in NSCLC, functioning as a critical stabilizer of numerous oncogenic client proteins, including receptor tyrosine kinases, transcription factors, and cell cycle regulators. In lung adenocarcinoma, HSP90 stabilizes mutant and wild-type EGFR, ALK fusion proteins, and other driver oncoproteins essential for tumor cell survival and proliferation (Trepel et al., 2010). The dependence of these oncoproteins on HSP90 for proper folding and stability renders HSP90 an attractive therapeutic target, particularly in tumors harboring driver mutations. Moreover, HSP90 inhibition has demonstrated potential in overcoming acquired resistance to EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (EGFR-TKIs) by simultaneously destabilizing multiple resistance-conferring proteins (Shimamura et al., 2012).

HSP70: Anti-Apoptotic Shield

HSP70 is significantly overexpressed in lung adenocarcinoma and serves as a critical anti-apoptotic factor that protects cancer cells from programmed cell death induced by chemotherapy, radiation, and targeted therapies. HSP70 interferes with both intrinsic and extrinsic apoptotic pathways by preventing caspase activation, inhibiting apoptosome formation, and modulating the function of pro-apoptotic proteins (Schmitt et al., 2007). Elevated HSP70 expression correlates with poor prognosis, advanced disease stage, and reduced overall survival in lung cancer patients, establishing it as both a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target.

HSP60: Metabolic Reprogramming and Survival

HSP60, primarily localized in mitochondria but also found in the cytoplasm of cancer cells, plays essential roles in mitochondrial protein folding, metabolic reprogramming, and anti-apoptotic signaling. In lung adenocarcinoma, HSP60 supports the metabolic adaptations required for rapid tumor growth and survival under nutrient-deprived and hypoxic conditions (Chun et al., 2010). The aberrant expression of HSP60 in the cytoplasm and cell surface of cancer cells facilitates immune evasion and promotes tumor progression and metastasis.

HSPs and Therapeutic Resistance

Collectively, HSP60, HSP70, and HSP90 contribute to chemoresistance, radioresistance, and resistance to targeted therapies through multiple mechanisms, including enhanced DNA repair, protection from drug-induced apoptosis, and stabilization of mutated oncoproteins (Calderwood and Gong, 2016). The compensatory upregulation of HSPs in response to cellular stress and therapeutic interventions represents a significant challenge in cancer treatment, highlighting the potential value of multi-HSP targeting strategies or combination approaches with conventional therapies.

EGFR/PI3K/AKT1 Signaling Axis: Central Hub of Oncogenic Signaling

The epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)/phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/AKT1 signaling pathway constitutes a central oncogenic axis in lung adenocarcinoma, governing critical cellular processes including proliferation, survival, metabolism, and resistance to apoptosis. Activating mutations in EGFR, particularly exon 19 deletions and the L858R point mutation, occur in approximately 10-15% of Caucasian and 30-50% of Asian NSCLC patients, making EGFR the most frequently mutated oncogene in lung adenocarcinoma.

EGFR Activation and Downstream Signaling

Upon ligand binding or constitutive activation by oncogenic mutations, EGFR undergoes autophosphorylation and recruits adaptor proteins that activate multiple downstream signaling cascades, most notably the PI3K/AKT and RAS/RAF/MEK/ERK pathways. PI3K phosphorylates phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate (PIP₂) to generate phosphatidylinositol-3,4,5-trisphosphate (PIP₃), which serves as a docking site for AKT1 and its activating kinase PDK1. Phosphorylated AKT1 subsequently activates numerous downstream effectors, including mTOR, MDM2, GSK3β, and FOXO transcription factors, thereby promoting cell survival, protein synthesis, glucose metabolism, and cell cycle progression while inhibiting apoptosis.

PI3K/AKT1 Pathway in EGFR-TKI Resistance

Despite initial dramatic responses to EGFR-TKIs such as gefitinib, erlotinib, and osimertinib, virtually all patients develop acquired resistance within 9-14 months of treatment initiation. Accumulating evidence demonstrates that sustained activation of the PI3K/AKT1 pathway represents a convergent feature of acquired EGFR-TKI resistance across diverse resistance mechanisms (Eberlein et al., 2015). PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway alterations are commonly detected in NSCLC patients following progression on EGFR-TKI therapy, contributing to treatment failure through bypass signaling mechanisms that maintain cell survival despite EGFR inhibition.

Mechanistically, resistance-associated proteins such as AXL receptor tyrosine kinase sense cellular stress and DNA damage, enabling cancer cells to adapt under EGFR-TKI treatment pressure. Additionally, Aurora B kinase (AURKB) continues to phosphorylate and degrade BIM, a pro-apoptotic BH3-only protein, thereby suppressing apoptosis even when EGFR is pharmacologically inhibited. The persistent activation of AKT1 through alternative receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs), loss of PTEN tumor suppressor function, or activating mutations in PIK3CA effectively bypasses EGFR inhibition and sustains oncogenic signaling.

Therapeutic Implications and Combination Strategies

The central role of the PI3K/AKT1 pathway in both oncogenic signaling and therapeutic resistance has prompted extensive investigation of combination strategies targeting multiple nodes within this network. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that dual inhibition of EGFR and AKT induces synergistic growth inhibition and apoptosis in EGFR-TKI-resistant models (Eberlein et al., 2015). Similarly, combinations of EGFR-TKIs with mTOR inhibitors such as everolimus have shown promise in overcoming resistance in clinical trials. However, the substantial tumor heterogeneity observed both within individual tumors and between primary and metastatic sites, coupled with the presence of multiple concurrent resistance mechanisms, necessitates personalized and adaptive treatment approaches guided by comprehensive molecular profiling and continuous monitoring through liquid biopsy technologies.

Mechanistic Convergence: Selenium Compounds, HSPs, and EGFR/PI3K/AKT1 Signaling

The therapeutic potential of selenadiazole derivatives with imino and sulphone functional groups in lung adenocarcinoma may be understood through their capacity to simultaneously modulate multiple interconnected pathways critical for cancer cell survival. Selenium compounds exert anticancer effects through redox modulation, generating reactive oxygen species that preferentially accumulate in cancer cells due to their elevated baseline oxidative stress. This ROS generation can trigger oxidative damage to proteins, lipids, and DNA, ultimately inducing cancer cell death.

Importantly, HSPs are exquisitely sensitive to oxidative stress and redox imbalance. Selenium-induced ROS accumulation may overwhelm the protective capacity of HSP60, HSP70, and HSP90, leading to protein misfolding, aggregation, and activation of apoptotic pathways. Furthermore, selenium compounds have been shown to modulate the expression and activity of heat shock transcription factors (HSFs), the master regulators of HSP expression, potentially disrupting the compensatory upregulation of HSPs that typically occurs in response to therapeutic stress.

The EGFR/PI3K/AKT1 signaling axis is also susceptible to selenium-mediated modulation. Selenium compounds can alter the thiol/disulfide balance of critical cysteine residues in EGFR, PI3K, and AKT1, affecting their phosphorylation status, protein-protein interactions, and enzymatic activities. Additionally, selenium-induced ROS generation activates stress-responsive kinases and phosphatases that can antagonize PI3K/AKT1 signaling. The ability of selenium compounds to modulate transcription factors such as NF-κB and p53 further contributes to their multi-targeted anticancer effects, as these transcription factors regulate the expression of genes involved in apoptosis, cell cycle control, and therapeutic resistance.

The integration of imino and sulphone functional groups into selenadiazole scaffolds may enhance these effects through improved cellular uptake, selective accumulation in tumor tissues, and controlled release of bioactive selenium species such as methylselenol under the hypoxic conditions characteristic of solid tumors. The selective cytotoxicity observed with these hybrid molecules suggests that they exploit fundamental differences between cancer and normal cells, including elevated oxidative stress, dependence on HSP-mediated protein homeostasis, and addiction to oncogenic signaling pathways.

Rationale and Objectives

Given the critical roles of HSP60, HSP70, and HSP90 in maintaining protein homeostasis and supporting oncogenic signaling in lung adenocarcinoma, and the central importance of the EGFR/PI3K/AKT1 pathway in tumor progression and therapeutic resistance, there exists a compelling rationale for investigating selenadiazole derivatives with imino and sulphone functional groups as multi-targeted anticancer agents. The present study aims to elucidate the molecular mechanisms by which these selenium-containing compounds modulate HSP expression and function, disrupt EGFR/PI3K/AKT1 signaling, and induce selective cytotoxicity in lung adenocarcinoma cells. By comprehensively characterizing the anticancer activities of these novel compounds and their effects on key molecular targets, this research seeks to establish a foundation for their potential clinical development as innovative therapeutic agents for lung adenocarcinoma, particularly in the context of overcoming resistance to current standard-of-care treatments.
