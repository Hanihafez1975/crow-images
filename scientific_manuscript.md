# pH and Time-Dependent Anticancer Effects of Chitosan-Encapsulated Selenadiazole Derivative on MCF7 Breast Cancer Cells: Modulation of PI3K/AKT Pathway and Apoptotic Signaling

## Abstract

**Background:** Breast cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality in women worldwide. The development of pH-responsive drug delivery systems targeting the acidic tumor microenvironment represents a promising therapeutic strategy. Selenadiazole derivatives have shown anticancer potential, but their efficacy can be enhanced through chitosan encapsulation.

**Objective:** This study investigated the pH and time-dependent effects of chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole derivative on MCF7 breast cancer cells, focusing on drug release kinetics and modulation of key molecular pathways including PI3K/AKT signaling, apoptosis, and growth factor expression.

**Methods:** MCF7 cells were treated with chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole derivative under three pH conditions (5.4, 6.5, and 7.4) representing acidic tumor microenvironment, physiological, and alkaline conditions, respectively. Treatments were evaluated at 12, 24, and 36 hours. Drug release percentage was measured, and expression levels of PI3K, AKT1, TGF-β, Caspase-3, Caspase-9, FGF2, and P53 were quantified using qRT-PCR. All experiments were performed in triplicate.

**Results:** Drug release was significantly pH-dependent, with maximum release at acidic pH 5.4 (80.8 ± 2.1% at 12h) compared to pH 7.4 (54.2 ± 1.6% at 12h, p < 0.001). Acidic pH conditions induced robust apoptotic responses with elevated Caspase-3 (3.53 ± 0.01 fold at pH 5.4 vs 1.23 ± 0.01 fold at pH 7.4, p < 0.001) and Caspase-9 (3.22 ± 0.01 fold at pH 5.4 vs 1.32 ± 0.001 fold at pH 7.4, p < 0.001) expression at 12h. Conversely, PI3K and AKT1 expression increased with time and at neutral to alkaline pH, suggesting compensatory survival pathway activation. P53 expression peaked at 24h under acidic conditions (3.04 ± 0.001 fold), coordinating with apoptotic signaling. Strong negative correlations were observed between apoptotic markers and survival pathway components (r = -0.97 for Caspase-3 vs AKT1, p < 0.001).

**Conclusions:** Chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole derivative demonstrates pH-responsive drug release with preferential activity in acidic tumor microenvironments. The formulation effectively induces apoptosis through caspase activation and P53 upregulation while suppressing PI3K/AKT survival signaling under acidic conditions. These findings support the therapeutic potential of this pH-responsive nanoformulation for targeted breast cancer treatment.

**Keywords:** Breast cancer, MCF7 cells, chitosan nanoparticles, selenadiazole, pH-responsive drug delivery, PI3K/AKT pathway, apoptosis, tumor microenvironment

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background

Breast cancer is the most frequently diagnosed cancer among women globally, accounting for approximately 30% of all female cancers and representing a significant public health challenge (1, 2). Despite advances in early detection and treatment, metastatic breast cancer remains largely incurable, with 5-year survival rates dropping dramatically for advanced-stage disease (3). The MCF7 cell line, derived from human breast adenocarcinoma, serves as a widely used model for studying estrogen receptor-positive (ER+) breast cancer, which comprises approximately 70% of all breast cancer cases (4).

### 1.2 Tumor Microenvironment and pH-Responsive Drug Delivery

The tumor microenvironment exhibits distinct physicochemical characteristics that differentiate it from normal tissue, including hypoxia, elevated interstitial pressure, and acidic extracellular pH (5, 6). Tumor acidosis, with pH values ranging from 6.0 to 6.8 compared to physiological pH of 7.4, results from increased glycolytic metabolism (Warburg effect) and poor perfusion (7, 8). This acidic microenvironment promotes tumor invasion, metastasis, and resistance to conventional therapies (9).

pH-responsive drug delivery systems exploit this pH gradient to achieve selective drug release in tumor tissues while minimizing systemic toxicity (10, 11). Chitosan, a biocompatible and biodegradable polysaccharide derived from chitin, has emerged as an attractive carrier for pH-responsive formulations due to its pH-dependent solubility, mucoadhesive properties, and inherent anticancer activity (12, 13).

### 1.3 Selenadiazole Derivatives as Anticancer Agents

Selenium-containing compounds have demonstrated significant anticancer potential through multiple mechanisms including induction of apoptosis, cell cycle arrest, inhibition of angiogenesis, and modulation of redox homeostasis (14, 15). Selenadiazole derivatives, characterized by a five-membered heterocyclic ring containing selenium and nitrogen atoms, exhibit enhanced bioavailability and reduced toxicity compared to inorganic selenium compounds (16, 17). Previous studies have shown that selenadiazole derivatives can induce oxidative stress, activate apoptotic pathways, and inhibit cancer cell proliferation (18, 19).

### 1.4 PI3K/AKT Signaling Pathway in Cancer

The phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/AKT pathway is one of the most frequently dysregulated signaling cascades in human cancers, including breast cancer (20, 21). This pathway regulates critical cellular processes including cell survival, proliferation, metabolism, and angiogenesis (22). Aberrant activation of PI3K/AKT signaling promotes cancer cell survival by inhibiting apoptosis through phosphorylation of pro-apoptotic proteins and activation of anti-apoptotic factors (23). Consequently, the PI3K/AKT pathway represents a key therapeutic target in cancer treatment (24).

### 1.5 Apoptotic Signaling and Tumor Suppression

Apoptosis, or programmed cell death, serves as a critical defense mechanism against cancer development (25). The intrinsic (mitochondrial) apoptotic pathway is regulated by the Bcl-2 family of proteins and executed by caspases, particularly Caspase-9 (initiator) and Caspase-3 (executioner) (26, 27). The tumor suppressor protein P53, often termed the "guardian of the genome," plays a central role in coordinating cellular responses to stress, including apoptosis induction (28, 29). Approximately 30% of breast cancers harbor P53 mutations, highlighting its importance in breast cancer biology (30).

### 1.6 Study Rationale and Objectives

While chitosan nanoparticles and selenadiazole derivatives have been independently studied for anticancer applications, their combination in a pH-responsive formulation has not been comprehensively characterized. Furthermore, the molecular mechanisms underlying their anticancer effects, particularly regarding PI3K/AKT pathway modulation and apoptotic signaling in the context of pH and time-dependent responses, remain incompletely understood.

**The objectives of this study were to:**

1. Characterize the pH and time-dependent drug release kinetics of chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole derivative
2. Evaluate the effects on PI3K/AKT survival pathway activation
3. Assess apoptotic pathway induction through Caspase-3, Caspase-9, and P53 expression
4. Examine growth factor modulation (TGF-β and FGF2)
5. Elucidate correlations between molecular markers to understand integrated pathway responses

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Cell Culture

MCF7 human breast adenocarcinoma cells were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA). Cells were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. Cultures were maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. Cells were passaged at 80-90% confluence using 0.25% trypsin-EDTA solution.

### 2.2 Preparation of Chitosan-Encapsulated Selenadiazole Derivative

Chitosan (medium molecular weight, 75-85% deacetylated) was dissolved in 1% acetic acid solution to prepare a 2 mg/mL stock solution. Selenadiazole derivative was synthesized according to previously published methods (31). Chitosan nanoparticles encapsulating selenadiazole derivative were prepared using ionic gelation method with sodium tripolyphosphate (TPP) as the crosslinking agent. The nanoparticle formulation was characterized for size, zeta potential, and encapsulation efficiency using dynamic light scattering and UV-visible spectrophotometry.

### 2.3 pH-Responsive Drug Release Study

Drug release kinetics were evaluated under three pH conditions: pH 5.4 (mimicking acidic tumor microenvironment), pH 6.5 (intermediate), and pH 7.4 (physiological pH). Chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole nanoparticles were suspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) adjusted to the respective pH values and incubated at 37°C with gentle agitation. At predetermined time points (12, 24, and 36 hours), aliquots were collected, centrifuged, and the supernatant was analyzed for released drug content using UV-visible spectrophotometry at 280 nm. Cumulative drug release percentage was calculated relative to total drug content. All experiments were performed in triplicate.

### 2.4 Cell Treatment

MCF7 cells were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 2 × 10⁵ cells per well and allowed to adhere overnight. Culture medium was then replaced with DMEM adjusted to pH 5.4, 6.5, or 7.4 using HCl or NaOH, supplemented with chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole derivative at a concentration corresponding to the IC₅₀ value determined in preliminary dose-response experiments. Cells were treated for 12, 24, or 36 hours under each pH condition. Each experimental condition was performed in triplicate.

### 2.5 RNA Extraction and Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Following treatment, total RNA was extracted from MCF7 cells using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer's protocol. RNA concentration and purity were assessed using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). First-strand cDNA was synthesized from 1 μg of total RNA using a reverse transcription kit with oligo(dT) primers.

Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using SYBR Green master mix on a QuantStudio Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems). Gene-specific primers were designed for PI3K (PIK3CA), AKT1, TGF-β (TGFB1), Caspase-3 (CASP3), Caspase-9 (CASP9), FGF2, and P53 (TP53). GAPDH served as the internal reference gene for normalization. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2^(-ΔΔCt) method and expressed as fold change relative to untreated control cells. All qRT-PCR reactions were performed in triplicate.

### 2.6 Statistical Analysis

All data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM) from three independent experiments. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's post-hoc test was used to compare multiple groups. Two-way ANOVA was employed to assess the effects of pH and time as independent variables. Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated to evaluate relationships between molecular markers. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 pH-Responsive Drug Release Kinetics

The chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole derivative demonstrated pronounced pH-responsive drug release behavior (Figure 1). At acidic pH 5.4, drug release was significantly higher across all time points compared to neutral and alkaline conditions. At 12 hours, cumulative release reached 80.8 ± 2.1% at pH 5.4, compared to 62.7 ± 0.8% at pH 6.5 and 54.2 ± 1.6% at pH 7.4 (p < 0.001 for pH 5.4 vs pH 7.4). This pH-dependent release pattern persisted at 24 hours (80.1 ± 1.5% at pH 5.4 vs 56.5 ± 1.2% at pH 7.4, p < 0.001) and 36 hours (70.5 ± 0.7% at pH 5.4 vs 55.9 ± 0.7% at pH 7.4, p < 0.001).

Time-dependent analysis revealed that at pH 5.4, drug release was rapid initially and plateaued after 12 hours, with a slight decrease observed at 36 hours (p < 0.01), possibly due to drug degradation or re-adsorption. At pH 6.5, release remained relatively stable across time points (p > 0.05). At pH 7.4, release showed minimal time-dependent variation, maintaining sustained release around 55% throughout the 36-hour period.

One-way ANOVA confirmed significant effects of pH on drug release at each time point (12h: F = 73.06, p < 0.0001; 24h: F = 94.69, p < 0.0001; 36h: F = 124.01, p < 0.0001). The pH-responsive release profile validates the formulation's potential for selective drug delivery in acidic tumor microenvironments.

### 3.2 PI3K Expression

PI3K expression exhibited complex pH and time-dependent regulation (Figure 2A-B). At acidic pH 5.4, PI3K expression was relatively suppressed at 12 hours (0.531 ± 0.012 fold) but progressively increased with time, reaching 0.596 ± 0.008 fold at 24 hours and 0.794 ± 0.004 fold at 36 hours (p < 0.001 for time effect). This time-dependent upregulation was even more pronounced at pH 6.5 (12h: 0.434 ± 0.010 fold; 24h: 0.609 ± 0.007 fold; 36h: 0.869 ± 0.008 fold, p < 0.001) and pH 7.4 (12h: 0.810 ± 0.010 fold; 24h: 0.847 ± 0.011 fold; 36h: 0.883 ± 0.019 fold, p < 0.05).

pH-dependent analysis revealed that at 12 hours, PI3K expression was highest at pH 7.4 and lowest at pH 6.5 (p < 0.001). At 24 hours, pH 7.4 maintained the highest expression (p < 0.001). By 36 hours, PI3K expression was elevated across all pH conditions, with pH 7.4 showing the highest levels (p < 0.01).

Two-way ANOVA demonstrated significant main effects of both time (F = 144.5, p < 0.0001) and pH (F = 89.3, p < 0.0001) on PI3K expression, with a significant interaction effect (F = 78.2, p < 0.0001), indicating that the time-dependent response varied across pH conditions.

### 3.3 AKT1 Expression

AKT1, a key downstream effector of PI3K, showed expression patterns largely paralleling PI3K (Figure 2C-D). At pH 5.4, AKT1 expression increased from 0.499 ± 0.007 fold at 12 hours to 0.537 ± 0.012 fold at 24 hours and 0.683 ± 0.009 fold at 36 hours (p < 0.001). At pH 6.5, a more dramatic time-dependent increase was observed (12h: 0.615 ± 0.005 fold; 24h: 0.723 ± 0.008 fold; 36h: 0.907 ± 0.010 fold, p < 0.001). At pH 7.4, AKT1 expression was highest at 12 hours (0.985 ± 0.005 fold) and remained elevated at 24 hours (0.903 ± 0.016 fold) and 36 hours (0.870 ± 0.012 fold, p < 0.05).

pH-dependent analysis showed that at all time points, AKT1 expression was significantly influenced by pH (12h: F = 1873.6, p < 0.0001; 24h: F = 221.1, p < 0.0001; 36h: F = 137.7, p < 0.0001). At 12 hours, pH 7.4 induced nearly 2-fold higher AKT1 expression compared to pH 5.4 (p < 0.001). This pH-dependent difference diminished at later time points as AKT1 expression increased under all conditions.

The strong positive correlation between PI3K and AKT1 expression (r = 0.815, p < 0.001) confirms coordinated regulation of this survival pathway.

### 3.4 TGF-β Expression

TGF-β, a multifunctional cytokine with context-dependent roles in cancer, demonstrated distinct regulatory patterns (Figure 4A, D). At pH 5.4, TGF-β expression increased progressively with time (12h: 0.464 ± 0.004 fold; 24h: 0.556 ± 0.014 fold; 36h: 0.816 ± 0.004 fold, p < 0.001). At pH 6.5, expression was elevated at 12 hours (0.752 ± 0.005 fold), slightly decreased at 24 hours (0.689 ± 0.005 fold), and increased again at 36 hours (0.779 ± 0.010 fold, p < 0.001). At pH 7.4, TGF-β expression remained relatively stable across time points (12h: 0.861 ± 0.008 fold; 24h: 0.870 ± 0.002 fold; 36h: 0.833 ± 0.014 fold, p > 0.05).

pH-dependent analysis revealed significant effects at all time points (12h: F = 1154.7, p < 0.0001; 24h: F = 345.9, p < 0.0001; 36h: F = 7.0, p < 0.05). At early time points (12h and 24h), TGF-β expression was highest at pH 7.4 and lowest at pH 5.4. By 36 hours, pH differences were less pronounced.

TGF-β showed strong positive correlations with AKT1 (r = 0.840, p < 0.001) and FGF2 (r = 0.896, p < 0.001), suggesting coordinated regulation of growth and survival signaling.

### 3.5 Caspase-3 Expression

Caspase-3, the primary executioner caspase in apoptosis, exhibited robust pH and time-dependent regulation inversely related to survival pathway activation (Figure 3A-B). At pH 5.4, Caspase-3 expression was highest at 12 hours (3.528 ± 0.010 fold), decreased at 24 hours (3.340 ± 0.005 fold), and further declined at 36 hours (2.835 ± 0.014 fold, p < 0.001). This time-dependent decrease was also observed at pH 6.5 (12h: 2.829 ± 0.010 fold; 24h: 2.441 ± 0.008 fold; 36h: 2.040 ± 0.001 fold, p < 0.001) and pH 7.4 (12h: 1.234 ± 0.010 fold; 24h: 2.057 ± 0.009 fold; 36h: 1.907 ± 0.009 fold, p < 0.001).

Notably, at pH 7.4, Caspase-3 expression showed an initial increase from 12 to 24 hours before declining at 36 hours, suggesting a delayed apoptotic response under neutral pH conditions.

pH-dependent analysis demonstrated dramatic effects at all time points (12h: F = 14597.2, p < 0.0001; 24h: F = 7736.6, p < 0.0001; 36h: F = 2764.9, p < 0.0001). At 12 hours, Caspase-3 expression at pH 5.4 was approximately 3-fold higher than at pH 7.4 (p < 0.001), indicating robust apoptotic induction under acidic conditions.

Caspase-3 showed strong negative correlations with AKT1 (r = -0.969, p < 0.001) and FGF2 (r = -0.874, p < 0.001), and strong positive correlations with drug release (r = 0.907, p < 0.001) and Caspase-9 (r = 0.975, p < 0.001).

### 3.6 Caspase-9 Expression

Caspase-9, the initiator caspase of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway, exhibited expression patterns closely paralleling Caspase-3 (Figure 3C-D). At pH 5.4, Caspase-9 expression decreased with time (12h: 3.216 ± 0.013 fold; 24h: 2.990 ± 0.012 fold; 36h: 2.707 ± 0.005 fold, p < 0.001). Similar time-dependent decreases were observed at pH 6.5 (12h: 2.517 ± 0.012 fold; 24h: 2.305 ± 0.004 fold; 36h: 1.782 ± 0.002 fold, p < 0.001) and pH 7.4 (12h: 1.319 ± 0.001 fold; 24h: 1.980 ± 0.001 fold; 36h: 1.429 ± 0.001 fold, p < 0.001).

pH-dependent effects were highly significant at all time points (12h: F = 8575.4, p < 0.0001; 24h: F = 4939.7, p < 0.0001; 36h: F = 41001.5, p < 0.0001). At 12 hours, Caspase-9 expression at pH 5.4 was 2.4-fold higher than at pH 7.4 (p < 0.001).

The exceptionally strong correlation between Caspase-9 and Caspase-3 (r = 0.975, p < 0.001) confirms coordinated activation of the apoptotic cascade. Caspase-9 also showed strong negative correlations with AKT1 (r = -0.947, p < 0.001) and FGF2 (r = -0.885, p < 0.001).

### 3.7 FGF2 Expression

Fibroblast growth factor 2 (FGF2), a pro-angiogenic and pro-survival factor, demonstrated pH and time-dependent upregulation (Figure 4B, E). At pH 5.4, FGF2 expression increased progressively with time (12h: 0.423 ± 0.0001 fold; 24h: 0.470 ± 0.001 fold; 36h: 0.645 ± 0.002 fold, p < 0.001). More dramatic increases were observed at pH 6.5 (12h: 0.740 ± 0.001 fold; 24h: 0.751 ± 0.001 fold; 36h: 0.839 ± 0.001 fold, p < 0.001) and pH 7.4 (12h: 0.822 ± 0.002 fold; 24h: 0.907 ± 0.003 fold; 36h: 0.865 ± 0.001 fold, p < 0.001).

pH-dependent analysis revealed highly significant effects at all time points (12h: F = 45912.2, p < 0.0001; 24h: F = 16122.5, p < 0.0001; 36h: F = 10320.7, p < 0.0001). FGF2 expression was consistently higher at neutral to alkaline pH compared to acidic pH.

FGF2 showed strong positive correlations with AKT1 (r = 0.891, p < 0.001) and TGF-β (r = 0.896, p < 0.001), and strong negative correlations with drug release (r = -0.961, p < 0.001), Caspase-3 (r = -0.874, p < 0.001), and P53 (r = -0.873, p < 0.001).

### 3.8 P53 Expression

The tumor suppressor P53 exhibited unique temporal and pH-dependent regulation (Figure 4C, F). At pH 5.4, P53 expression increased from 12 hours (2.811 ± 0.002 fold) to peak at 24 hours (3.041 ± 0.001 fold), then decreased at 36 hours (1.980 ± 0.001 fold, p < 0.001). At pH 6.5, P53 was elevated at 12 hours (2.040 ± 0.001 fold), peaked at 24 hours (2.649 ± 0.001 fold), and declined at 36 hours (1.741 ± 0.001 fold, p < 0.001). At pH 7.4, P53 expression was relatively stable across time points (12h: 1.850 ± 0.001 fold; 24h: 1.451 ± 0.001 fold; 36h: 1.519 ± 0.001 fold, p < 0.001).

The peak P53 expression at 24 hours under acidic conditions suggests a coordinated stress response that precedes or accompanies maximal apoptotic activation. pH-dependent effects were highly significant at all time points (12h: F = 184216.3, p < 0.0001; 24h: F = 686161.3, p < 0.0001; 36h: F = 75537.8, p < 0.0001).

P53 showed strong positive correlations with drug release (r = 0.824, p < 0.001), Caspase-3 (r = 0.751, p < 0.001), and Caspase-9 (r = 0.775, p < 0.001), and strong negative correlations with AKT1 (r = -0.810, p < 0.001), TGF-β (r = -0.900, p < 0.001), and FGF2 (r = -0.873, p < 0.001).

### 3.9 Correlation Analysis

Comprehensive Pearson correlation analysis revealed extensive interconnections among measured parameters (Figure 5B). The strongest positive correlations were observed between:
- Caspase-3 and Caspase-9 (r = 0.975, p < 0.001)
- Drug release and Caspase-9 (r = 0.917, p < 0.001)
- Drug release and Caspase-3 (r = 0.907, p < 0.001)
- AKT1 and TGF-β (r = 0.840, p < 0.001)
- PI3K and AKT1 (r = 0.815, p < 0.001)

The strongest negative correlations were observed between:
- Drug release and FGF2 (r = -0.961, p < 0.001)
- Caspase-3 and AKT1 (r = -0.969, p < 0.001)
- Caspase-9 and AKT1 (r = -0.947, p < 0.001)
- TGF-β and P53 (r = -0.900, p < 0.001)
- AKT1 and FGF2 (r = 0.891, p < 0.001)

These correlation patterns reveal two antagonistic molecular programs: (1) an apoptotic program characterized by high drug release, elevated caspase and P53 expression, predominant under acidic pH; and (2) a survival program characterized by PI3K/AKT activation, growth factor upregulation, and suppressed apoptosis, predominant under neutral to alkaline pH.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 pH-Responsive Drug Delivery and Tumor Targeting

This study demonstrates that chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole derivative exhibits pronounced pH-responsive drug release, with maximum release occurring at acidic pH 5.4, closely mimicking the tumor microenvironment. The 1.5-fold higher drug release at pH 5.4 compared to pH 7.4 at 12 hours validates the formulation's potential for selective tumor targeting while minimizing systemic exposure.

The pH-responsive behavior of chitosan nanoparticles is attributed to protonation of amino groups at acidic pH, leading to increased electrostatic repulsion, nanoparticle swelling, and enhanced drug release (32, 33). This mechanism provides a passive targeting strategy that exploits the inherent acidity of solid tumors resulting from aerobic glycolysis and poor perfusion (34). Previous studies have demonstrated that pH-responsive nanoparticles can achieve 2-3 fold higher drug accumulation in tumors compared to normal tissues (35, 36).

The sustained release profile observed over 36 hours suggests potential for reduced dosing frequency and improved patient compliance. However, the slight decrease in cumulative release at 36 hours under acidic conditions warrants further investigation into drug stability and potential degradation pathways.

### 4.2 Suppression of PI3K/AKT Survival Pathway Under Acidic Conditions

The PI3K/AKT pathway is frequently hyperactivated in breast cancer and promotes cell survival, proliferation, and therapy resistance (37, 38). Our results demonstrate that acidic pH conditions, which promote maximum drug release, are associated with relatively suppressed PI3K and AKT1 expression, particularly at early time points. Conversely, neutral to alkaline pH conditions, which limit drug release, permit robust PI3K/AKT activation.

The time-dependent increase in PI3K/AKT expression across all pH conditions suggests a compensatory survival response to treatment stress. This adaptive response is consistent with previous reports of feedback activation of survival pathways following anticancer therapy (39, 40). The more pronounced PI3K/AKT upregulation at pH 6.5 and 7.4 may reflect reduced drug-mediated suppression under these conditions.

The strong negative correlations between drug release and PI3K/AKT expression (r = -0.609 for PI3K, r = -0.905 for AKT1) support a direct inhibitory effect of the selenadiazole derivative on this pathway. Selenium compounds have been previously shown to inhibit PI3K/AKT signaling through multiple mechanisms including PTEN activation, direct PI3K inhibition, and disruption of AKT membrane localization (41, 42).

### 4.3 Robust Apoptotic Induction Through Caspase Activation

The dramatic upregulation of Caspase-3 and Caspase-9 under acidic pH conditions, correlating with maximum drug release, demonstrates potent pro-apoptotic activity of the chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole derivative. The 3-fold higher Caspase-3 expression at pH 5.4 compared to pH 7.4 at 12 hours indicates rapid and robust apoptotic induction in the acidic tumor microenvironment.

The exceptionally strong correlation between Caspase-9 and Caspase-3 (r = 0.975) confirms activation of the intrinsic (mitochondrial) apoptotic pathway, where Caspase-9 initiates the caspase cascade leading to Caspase-3 activation and execution of apoptosis (43, 44). This pathway is triggered by mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization, cytochrome c release, and apoptosome formation (45).

Selenium compounds, including selenadiazole derivatives, are known to induce apoptosis through multiple mechanisms including reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation, mitochondrial dysfunction, endoplasmic reticulum stress, and modulation of Bcl-2 family proteins (46, 47). The acidic pH may enhance these effects through increased drug bioavailability and potential pH-dependent modulation of cellular redox status (48).

The time-dependent decrease in caspase expression at pH 5.4 and 6.5 may reflect depletion of viable cells capable of expressing these markers, or activation of negative feedback mechanisms. The delayed caspase activation observed at pH 7.4 (peak at 24h rather than 12h) suggests slower apoptotic kinetics under neutral pH conditions, consistent with reduced drug release.

### 4.4 P53-Mediated Tumor Suppression

The peak P53 expression at 24 hours under acidic conditions represents a critical finding, as P53 serves as a master regulator of cellular stress responses including apoptosis, cell cycle arrest, and DNA repair (49, 50). MCF7 cells harbor wild-type P53, making them responsive to P53-mediated tumor suppression (51).

The temporal pattern of P53 expression, peaking at 24 hours and declining at 36 hours, suggests a biphasic response where initial stress sensing and transcriptional activation are followed by resolution or cell death. The strong positive correlations between P53 and caspases (r = 0.751 for Caspase-3, r = 0.775 for Caspase-9) indicate coordinated regulation of apoptotic signaling.

P53 can induce apoptosis through transcriptional activation of pro-apoptotic genes including BAX, PUMA, and NOXA, as well as transcriptional repression of anti-apoptotic genes like BCL2 (52, 53). Additionally, P53 can directly interact with mitochondrial Bcl-2 family proteins to promote mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (54).

The strong negative correlation between P53 and TGF-β (r = -0.900) is particularly interesting, as TGF-β can suppress P53 activity through multiple mechanisms including SMAD-mediated transcriptional interference and promotion of P53 degradation (55, 56). This antagonistic relationship may represent a critical determinant of cell fate decisions.

### 4.5 Growth Factor Modulation and Survival Signaling

FGF2 and TGF-β, both implicated in cancer progression, showed pH-dependent upregulation with higher expression at neutral to alkaline pH. FGF2 promotes angiogenesis, cell proliferation, and survival through activation of MAPK and PI3K/AKT pathways (57, 58). The strong positive correlation between FGF2 and AKT1 (r = 0.891) supports coordinated activation of survival signaling.

TGF-β exhibits context-dependent roles in cancer, acting as a tumor suppressor in early stages but promoting invasion, metastasis, and immune evasion in advanced disease (59, 60). In MCF7 cells, TGF-β has been shown to induce growth arrest at low concentrations but promote epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) at higher concentrations (61).

The upregulation of FGF2 and TGF-β under conditions of reduced drug release (neutral to alkaline pH) suggests these factors may contribute to therapy resistance. Conversely, their suppression under acidic conditions may enhance therapeutic efficacy. The strong negative correlations between these growth factors and apoptotic markers support antagonistic regulation of survival versus death pathways.

### 4.6 Integrated Pathway Analysis and Therapeutic Implications

The correlation analysis reveals a clear dichotomy between two molecular programs: an apoptotic program (high drug release, elevated caspases and P53, suppressed PI3K/AKT and growth factors) predominant under acidic pH, and a survival program (low drug release, elevated PI3K/AKT and growth factors, suppressed apoptosis) predominant under neutral to alkaline pH.

This pH-dependent molecular switch has important therapeutic implications. The preferential activation of apoptotic pathways in acidic tumor microenvironments suggests that chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole derivative may achieve selective tumor cell killing while sparing normal tissues at physiological pH. This selectivity could reduce systemic toxicity and improve the therapeutic index.

However, the time-dependent upregulation of PI3K/AKT survival signaling, even under acidic conditions, suggests potential for adaptive resistance. Combination strategies targeting both apoptotic induction and survival pathway inhibition may be necessary for optimal therapeutic efficacy. PI3K/AKT inhibitors, currently in clinical development for breast cancer, could synergize with chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole derivative (62, 63).

The strong correlations between molecular markers also suggest potential biomarkers for treatment response. Baseline or early changes in PI3K/AKT, caspase, or P53 expression could predict therapeutic efficacy and guide personalized treatment strategies.

### 4.7 Limitations and Future Directions

This study has several limitations that should be addressed in future research. First, the in vitro model using MCF7 cells, while widely accepted, does not fully recapitulate the complexity of the tumor microenvironment including stromal cells, immune cells, and three-dimensional architecture. Future studies should employ 3D spheroid cultures, co-culture systems, and in vivo tumor models to validate these findings.

Second, while gene expression analysis provides valuable mechanistic insights, protein-level validation through Western blotting, immunofluorescence, or flow cytometry would strengthen the conclusions. Post-translational modifications, particularly phosphorylation of AKT and other signaling molecules, should be examined.

Third, functional assays including cell viability, apoptosis (Annexin V/PI staining, TUNEL assay), cell cycle analysis, and clonogenic survival would provide direct evidence of therapeutic efficacy. Fourth, the mechanisms underlying pH-responsive drug release and cellular uptake require further investigation using techniques such as confocal microscopy, transmission electron microscopy, and flow cytometry.

Fifth, the study focused on a single cell line (MCF7, ER+/HER2-). Validation in other breast cancer subtypes including triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) and HER2+ breast cancer would establish the generalizability of these findings. Sixth, investigation of additional molecular markers including Bcl-2 family proteins (BAX, BCL2, BID), autophagy markers (LC3, p62), and oxidative stress markers (ROS, glutathione) would provide a more comprehensive mechanistic understanding.

Finally, pharmacokinetic and biodistribution studies in animal models are essential to evaluate in vivo drug release, tumor accumulation, and systemic clearance. Toxicology studies would assess safety and identify potential adverse effects.

### 4.8 Clinical Relevance

The findings of this study have potential clinical relevance for breast cancer treatment. pH-responsive nanoparticle formulations represent a promising strategy for improving drug delivery to solid tumors while minimizing systemic toxicity (64, 65). Several pH-responsive nanoparticle systems are currently in preclinical and clinical development for various cancers (66).

Chitosan-based nanoparticles offer several advantages including biocompatibility, biodegradability, low immunogenicity, and ease of synthesis and modification (67, 68). Chitosan is generally recognized as safe (GRAS) by the FDA and has been used in various pharmaceutical and biomedical applications (69).

Selenadiazole derivatives represent a novel class of selenium-containing anticancer agents with potential advantages over inorganic selenium compounds including improved bioavailability, reduced toxicity, and enhanced anticancer activity (70). The combination of chitosan nanoparticles and selenadiazole derivatives leverages the strengths of both components to achieve pH-responsive, tumor-targeted drug delivery.

The molecular mechanisms identified in this study, particularly PI3K/AKT pathway suppression and caspase-mediated apoptosis induction, provide a rational basis for combination therapy strategies. PI3K/AKT inhibitors, currently in clinical trials for breast cancer, could potentially synergize with chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole derivative to overcome adaptive resistance mechanisms.

---

## 5. Conclusions

This study demonstrates that chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole derivative exhibits pH-responsive drug release with preferential activity in acidic tumor microenvironments. Under acidic conditions (pH 5.4), the formulation achieves maximum drug release (80.8%) and induces robust apoptotic responses characterized by elevated Caspase-3, Caspase-9, and P53 expression, while suppressing PI3K/AKT survival signaling. Conversely, neutral to alkaline pH conditions permit PI3K/AKT activation and growth factor upregulation while limiting drug release and apoptotic induction.

The strong correlations between molecular markers reveal antagonistic regulation of survival and death pathways, with pH serving as a critical determinant of cell fate. These findings support the therapeutic potential of chitosan-encapsulated selenadiazole derivative as a pH-responsive nanoformulation for targeted breast cancer treatment.

Future studies employing 3D culture systems, in vivo tumor models, and comprehensive molecular profiling will be essential to validate these findings and advance this formulation toward clinical translation. Combination strategies targeting both apoptotic induction and survival pathway inhibition may optimize therapeutic efficacy and overcome adaptive resistance mechanisms.
